TC3D-295 · distant terrain near the horizon measurement, no fix lanes

Does the weather composite give distant terrain near the horizon a sky distance (1,000 km)?

No. At this pose every terrain pixel has a real depth, and the composite decodes it to the right distance. A debug switch wrote the composite's raw depth as 24-bit colour, and then sceneDistance() in metres. Each row was compared with a CPU ray pick of the terrain (globe.pick) and with the ray's WGS84 ellipsoid hit. Neither the "no depth drawn" nor the "decoded wrong" outcome holds, so this section shows the measurement and no fix lanes.

Recommendation: close TC3D-295 as not reproduced, with this measurement as the pin. If a pale band on far terrain still bothers you at some weather, that band is Cesium's ground atmosphere (globe.showGroundAtmosphere / scene.fog), not the composite. It would be a new issue.

Worst case: the measurement covers EGLL at 88.8 m in four directions. A much higher camera (above the deck, where tiles near the horizon are coarser) was not measured.

TC3D-294 · BKN streaks along the horizon decision 5

Which change removes the white streaks of a BKN 274 m layer under an OVC 610 m deck at 10 SM?

Weather: BKN 274.3 m cov 0.7 + OVC 609.6 m cov 1, 10 SM. The cloud drift time is pinned so every lane sees the same cloud field. Row step is the largest luminance jump between adjacent rows (0–255, centre 200 columns) from 2° above the horizon down to 8 rows above it, the issue's metric (target ≤ 6), at drift 0 / 300 / 600 s. GPU is gpuCesiumMs from __tc3d.perf(), median and p95 over 300 frames, as the median of rounds 2–4 of four interleaved rounds. Stage off read 2.03 / 2.32 ms, so the stage's own cost is the lane's value minus 2.03. In round 1 every lane read near the stage-off value (timer lag after the stage rebuild), so round 1 is left out.

Finding: the three candidates in the issue (B, C, D) leave the streaks exactly as they are. Their row steps stay within 1.5 of today's at every drift, so the streaks are not undersampling. They are the BKN tops lit as if in full sun, seen edge-on through gaps, although an overcast deck sits above them. Lanes E and F test that cause.

TC3D-224 · fade shape below 10 SM decisions 1 and 3

How should the fog arrive when the METAR visibility drops, and when it crosses 10 SM?

Fog only (no cloud layers). The ease runs on a pinned clock stepped 0.1 s per frame, so the clips are exact whatever the capture speed (10 fps, real time). The chart is the mean luminance of canvas rows 300–340, centre 600 columns: the skyline and the terrain 15–43 km out.

  • A · today: 0 fog at 10 SM or more, the full METAR fog below it, applied on the next frame.
  • B · extinction fade: the fog's optical depth is scaled by a smoothstep from 0 at 10 SM to 1 at 8 SM, with a 6 s smoothstep time ease of the fog density.
  • C · visibility fade: the visibility is interpolated in log space from 100 SM-equivalent at 10 SM to the METAR value at 8 SM, with the same 6 s ease.
  • D · time ease only: today's mapping, with the 6 s ease.

10 SM → 6 SM METAR step at t = 0

3 SM → 1 SM METAR step at t = 0

Visibility gliding 10.5 → 8.5 SM over 8 s, no time ease (the shapes alone)

This is what weather interpolation produces, and the only case where B and C differ. The glide crosses 10 SM at t = 2 s.

The same glide with the 6 s smoothstep ease on top

Recommendation (shape): B, the extinction fade from 10 to 8 SM, plus a time ease for METAR steps. On a glide it fades the fog in from 10 SM with no pop and stays close to the METAR value (114 against 118 at 8.5 SM). C trails B through the whole band and is still 9 levels short of today's fog at 8.5 SM.

Recommendation (ease mechanics): do not restart a smoothstep on every target change. Either ease only METAR steps and pass the interpolated (gliding) visibility straight through, or use an ease that keeps its speed when the target moves (exponential or critically damped).

Worst case of B: between 8 and 10 SM the fog is weaker than the METAR says (at 9 SM, half the physical optical depth). Worst case of C: a residual haze of 100 SM-equivalent visibility just under 10 SM, and fog that builds late in the band, still well short of the METAR at 8.5 SM. Worst case of D: a crossing of 10 SM still steps the fog target, which only the ease hides.

TC3D-224 · ease time decision 3

How long should a METAR step take to show: 2 s, 6 s or 15 s (smoothstep)?

10 SM → 6 SM at t = 0, time ease only (lane D's mapping), clock stepped 0.2 s per frame (5 fps, real time).

2 s

Reads as a quick fade rather than a pop.

Worst case: still a visible sweep (7.9 levels per 0.2 s) when you happen to be looking.

6 s

A slow fade that is hard to catch happening. 90% after 4.7 s.

Worst case: the fog lags the METAR by about 5 s.

15 s

Imperceptible. 90% after 11.5 s.

Worst case: for about 10 s after a METAR the scene shows weather the controller has already read as changed.

Recommendation: 6 s smoothstep for METAR steps. It never moves more than 2.7 levels per 0.2 s and is done long before the next METAR (they refresh every 5 minutes).
