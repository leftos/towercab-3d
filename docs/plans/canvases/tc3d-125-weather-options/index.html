TC3D-125: weather stage options

Rendered mocks for decisions 1, 3/4, 6 and 7 of the TC3D-125 map (overcast leaves a clear-sky band at the horizon; dense fog renders dark teal). Every lane is a throwaway prototype on the Cesium stage of feat/cesium-weather at 3ac96df; the rebuild steps and the prototype diff are in RECIPE.md beside this page.

Baseline. EGLL, BKN 274.3 m (coverage 0.7) + OVC 609.6 m (coverage 1). Clock fixed at 13:00 UTC (sun high, daylight() = 1). Tower camera 88.8 m above the anchor ground (not 80 m), heading 274, pitch −3, FOV 45; canvas 1596×800, horizon on row 309. Headless Edge with a GPU, pnpm run vite:headless.

How to read the numbers. Strip: mean RGB of the rows 0.24–0.6° above the horizon (centre 200 columns), where the band sits. Row step: the largest luminance jump between two adjacent rows from 2° above the horizon down to 0.24° above it (0–255; the band's edge). Haze: 100×100 patch just below the horizon. Overhead: rows 2.6–3.8° above the horizon. Each is a mean over three frames.

Frame cost. perf().window is no use here: frameInterval sits at 16.7 ms (60 Hz cap) in every lane, gpuCesiumMs returned the same 5.63/8.2 ms in every lane (stale), and cesiumDraw wandered 2.4–4.8 ms with no lane pattern. The cost column is instead the wall time of one scene.render() plus a 1-pixel readPixels (which waits for the GPU), lanes interleaved over 8 rounds of 20 frames. Other builds were running on the machine, so compare lanes with each other, not with any frame budget.

A. Deck radius decision 1

How far should the overcast reach: today's flat 40 km, a curved deck at a fixed 100 km, or a curved deck sized to the horizon?

Curved means the slab follows the earth down by d²/2R (R = 6,371 km), with exact entry and exit along the ray. Steps scale with the radius (48 per 40 km). The edge fade stays at 75–100% of the radius in every lane. Horizon-derived radius for an 88.8 m camera and a 609.6 m OVC base: 121.8 km.

B. Top relief decisions 3 and 4

Seen from above the overcast, should the top stay flat or rise in billows, and how much?

Camera 1 km east of the tower at 1,524 m (5,000 ft) above the ground, heading 274; pitch −16 (as the spike's case 3c) and −6 (across the deck). 10 SM. All B lanes use the A3 curved 121.8 km deck so a deck edge does not distract. The base stays flat; a two-octave 2D value noise (5 km and 2 km cells) raises the top by up to the lane's share of the slab thickness (600 m for OVC, 450 m for BKN). Findings the map did not predict

  • At 5.96 SM the band is not sky: it is a fog-grey strip (158,168,178) under a darker, bluer deck. The radius does not change it (A1–A3 step 9–13); retuning the underside does (A3 + C2 step 3.7).
  • Dense fog at 1.3 SM is not dark teal on the Cesium path at 13:00 UTC: haze lum 152, chroma 13. scene.fog barely affects it (C4).
  • The flat-deck band at 10 SM measured about 0.6° (17–22 px), under the computed 1.05–1.3°: terrain covers its lowest part.
  • A 100 km curved deck leaves no visible sliver from the EGLL tower at 88.8 m; the computed 0.14° residual sits under the terrain.
  • The composite treats terrain within about 1° below the horizon as sky (D2's haze reaches it), so sceneDistance returns SKY_DISTANCE for those pixels. Cause unverified.
  • The white streaks of the BKN layer near the horizon (undersampled noise at grazing angles) appear in every A lane at 10 SM and are now the largest row step there.
