Display delay easing for a new VATSIM aircraft

An aircraft on final at 140 kt, seen from above. When its third report arrives (at 5 s here), its display delay eases from the 17 s start to the measured 15.15 s. While the delay shrinks, the aircraft is drawn faster than it flies. The faint marker is where it would be at a steady 140 kt. Each lane is one easing rate; the loop runs 30 s.

After vNAS is lost

One aircraft sends vNAS packets every second until t = 60 s, then only its VATSIM reports (every 15 s, at 7.4 s past each 15 s mark) reach the app, through t = 300 s. vNAS stays preferred for 30 s after its last packet, so the aircraft is drawn from vNAS data alone until t = 90 s. Each lane replays the store's delay logic (interval window, 150 ms jitter buffer, 200 ms extrapolation bump, reconciliation glide) with one change of rule. Orange means the aircraft is drawn ahead of the newest report it uses: the app is guessing from that report's speed, and the guess is corrected when the next report lands.

Taxi: 15 kt with three stops (slow at 1.5 kt/s from 75 s, 155 s and 245 s, hold 15 s, roll again). Approach: 170 kt slowing at 1 kt/s to 140 kt between 75 s and 105 s, then steady. The window follows the aircraft's present position; taxi marks every 25 m, approach marks every 200 m. Snap-back is the longest run of backward motion of the drawn aircraft; "at hand-over" counts the runs from the moment vNAS is dropped until 2 s after the first VATSIM report from then on (where C's step lands), "later" counts the runs after that. Worst guess is the largest gap between the drawn aircraft and where it really was at the drawn moment. Settled is the last moment after which the aircraft never guesses and its delay stays within 0.1 s of the final value. URL parameters: ?t=120 (paused at that time), &motion=approach, &stale=5.
