TC3D-369: when does an unlearned aircraft's provisional resting height leave the drawing?

A B77L with no learned resting height lands, gets a provisional height of 13.6 ft from its settled samples, and is drawn that much lower. On takeoff (constructed: roll at 13.6 ft raw, a 2.9 ft rotation hump, liftoff at 10 s, a heavy's climb to about 30 ft/s) the offset has to go at some point. Each lane plots the wheel height drawn against the true wheel height.

drawntrue

A. Drop at 25 ft (as built)

The provisional height leaves the drawing over 0.25 s once the raw height passes 25 ft: the aircraft jumps up 13.6 ft (4.1 m) at about 11 ft above the runway, just after liftoff, in full view of the tower. From then on it is drawn 13.6 ft above its true height, as every unlearned aircraft is drawn in the air today.

B. Fade out from 25 to 100 ft (recommended)

The offset shrinks in step with the climb, so the jump is spread over the first 75 ft of climb: the drawn climb is a little steeper for about 2 s and never jumps. Above 100 ft it is drawn 13.6 ft above its true height, as in A. A wrong provisional height (one learned on a level pass) is gone by 100 ft, before the next approach.

C. Keep it while airborne

The aircraft is drawn at its true height all the way up, as one with a learned resting height is. Worst case: a provisional height entered wrongly (a level pass under 16 ft) stays, and the aircraft is drawn up to 3.6 m low for the rest of the flight and enters contact early on its next approach.
